Creatine: how much

Pick one lane and stay in it for at least eight weeks. All three use plain creatine monohydrate — no need for the expensive forms.

General brain and muscle upkeep

5 g once daily

The standard dose. Saturates muscle reliably, but only nudges brain creatine. Good if you're taking it as background insurance.

Cognitive focus, moderateGood starting point

5 g twice daily

10 g a day, split morning and dinner. The brain takes up creatine slowly and needs more than muscle does, so higher totals are what move brain levels. Easier on the gut than 20 g.

Matching the Alzheimer's trial

10 g twice daily

20 g a day, exactly as the study ran it. This is a large dose — build up over two weeks rather than starting here, and drink noticeably more water.

What the research actually showed. The CABA pilot at the University of Kansas gave 20 g of creatine monohydrate daily for eight weeks, split into two 10 g doses, to about twenty people with Alzheimer's. Brain creatine rose 11%, and cognitive scores improved. But this was a single-arm pilot with no placebo group, run for two months. It shows the dose is tolerable and reaches the brain — it does not prove creatine treats or prevents dementia. Larger controlled trials are still pending.

Read before you start

No prescriptions in the picture, so the drug-interaction problems mostly drop away. These five still apply.

  • Methylene blue is not on the schedule on purpose. Your bottle is a bacteriostatic solution labeled for research use — it isn't sold or tested as something to swallow, and dosing by drops of a 1% solution has no margin for error. It also breaks down red blood cells in people with G6PD deficiency, which often goes undiagnosed until something triggers it. Separately: if you ever start an antidepressant, methylene blue and SSRIs together can cause serotonin syndrome. Worth a doctor's take before you add it.
  • Nattokinase and fish oil both slow clotting. Not a problem day to day, but stop both about two weeks before any surgery, dental extraction, or procedure. If you notice easy bruising, frequent nosebleeds, or cuts that keep bleeding, drop the nattokinase first.
  • Berberine can drop blood sugar even without diabetes in the mix. If you feel shaky or lightheaded an hour after a dose, you took it too far from food. It's also hard on the gut — many people run it 8 to 12 weeks, then take a few weeks off.
  • Creatine will raise your creatinine on a blood test. That's the supplement showing up, not a kidney problem, but tell whoever orders the labs so nobody chases a false alarm. Drink more water than usual, especially at 20 g a day.
  • Sea moss is a real iodine dose. Too much iodine can push a healthy thyroid in either direction. If you get fatigued, jittery, or notice neck swelling, that's the one to pull.

Check these against your labels

  • Serving sizes. I read your bottles from photos. Confirm the capsules-per-serving on the magnesium, sea moss, and berberine before you settle in.
  • Folate overlap. Between the B complex and the separate methylfolate you may be well above what you need. Worth a conversation if you weren't specifically told to take both.
  • Vitamin D amount. If you don't know your blood level, a 25-OH vitamin D test is the cheapest way to find out whether that capsule is right, too little, or unnecessary.
